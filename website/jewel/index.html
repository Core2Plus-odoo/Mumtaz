The things a great salesperson carries in their head — and that walk out of the door when they leave. Kept on the client, visible to the people who should see it, and to nobody else.

The interface · in design

A front end built for the counter, not for an ERP

Odoo 19 Community is the engine: stock, accounting, permissions and the approval rules all live there, and they work today. On top of it goes an interface designed for the people who actually use it — an associate with a client in front of them, and an owner with four minutes. These four screens are a clickable prototype, not the shipped product: nothing in them is wired to a database and every client is invented.

How a sale actually happens

From a moment on the calendar to a piece in a box

The moment arrives

An anniversary is three weeks out. A draft is prepared for the relationship manager — never sent automatically, and never to a Private or Royal client.

A tray is put together

Three to five pieces chosen for that client, in stock now, with the reasons and the live price beside each one.

The piece goes out

Each piece is serial-tracked with its own weights, karat, tag and certificate. You know which one left, with whom, and when it is due back.

The owner sees it

Rates waiting for approval, exchanges waiting for a second person, pieces overdue back, metal held and what it is worth — one screen of decisions.

AI teammates · in development

Four teammates for the client relationship

Not a chatbot bolted on the side. Four narrow agents that work the client book, each kept on a short leash and none of them allowed to produce a number. These are being built now and are not available yet — they are on the roadmap, described here so you can tell us if we are building the wrong ones.

Autonomy A–B · reads and explains
A Read B Recommend C Draft D Execute, with a named approval E Prohibited

Three rules hold whatever an agent is asked to do. The number always comes from the pricing engine at the approved rate, never from a model. An agent runs as the person who invoked it, so it can never see a client that person cannot. And restricted clients are capped lower still: their data never enters a model request, and no automated message is ever sent to them.

Underneath all of it

One approved rate, and every price computed from it

The client book only works if the number beside the piece is right. That is the part that is finished and tested hardest.

A rate needs two people Built

The person who enters the day's rate cannot approve it. Nor can anyone work around the buttons: the state cannot be written directly, by any user or by system code.

No price without one Built

With no approved rate in force the system refuses to price, rather than showing a figure someone might trust. Prices hold at the last approved rate.

Old gold is computed Built

Melt loss and buy-back margin applied at the approved rate, the rate used saved on the record, and a second person above the limit you set. Nobody types the payout.

Four modules, on standard Odoo 19

No fork and no Enterprise licence. They install on Odoo 19 Community and extend the standard models rather than replacing them, so stock, contacts and accounting stay where Odoo keeps them.
